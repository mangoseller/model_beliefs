# Plan: supplement behavioral latent beliefs with residual-stream activations

## Question

Test whether a small language model contains a shared, behaviorally relevant internal signal that is not fully inferable from its external responses. Compare the paper's behavioral latent-belief model with an extension receiving the same external responses plus one linear projection of residual-stream activations.

Reference: [Smolin and Wilder, *Beliefs and Behavior in Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07943), especially the shared-threshold specification in Equation 1.

Improved prediction would support the claim that internals contain additional information about behavior. Cross-framing generalization would strengthen the interpretation as a shared belief. Neither result alone establishes that models have beliefs in a stronger philosophical sense, and a comparison across model sizes would require a subsequent experiment.

## First model: a deterministic activation shift

For case $i$ and decision $j$, define:

- $h_i^{(\ell)}$: residual-stream activation at layer $\ell$, extracted from a fixed, decision-independent prompt and token position.
- $\widetilde h_i$: activation centered using the fitting-set mean.
- $u_i$: the original latent belief expressed in log-odds, with prior $p_0(u)$.
- $r_i = w^\top \widetilde h_i$: a single learned activation projection shared across decisions.
- $s_i = u_i + r_i$: effective belief log-odds.
- $\tau_j$: the decision threshold implied by the instruction or payoffs.

The augmented behavioral model is

$$
u_i \sim p_0(u), \qquad s_i = u_i + w^\top \widetilde h_i,
$$

$$
q_{ij}(u_i,h_i)
= P(a_{ij}=1\mid u_i,h_i)
= \operatorname{sigmoid}\left(
\alpha+\beta\left[u_i+w^\top\widetilde h_i-\operatorname{logit}(\tau_j)\right]
\right), \qquad \beta>0.
$$

For binary responses, the likelihood contribution is

$$
p(a_{ij}\mid u_i,h_i)
=q_{ij}^{a_{ij}}(1-q_{ij})^{1-a_{ij}}.
$$

Responses are conditionally independent given the effective latent belief, as in the baseline. If numerical probability reports or ordinal responses are retained, evaluate their existing likelihoods at the effective belief $\operatorname{sigmoid}(s_i)$ as well. The shift must affect the shared belief, not just the binary decision channel.

There is no additional activation-noise parameter in this first version. Uncertainty remains in $u_i$ and in the behavioral likelihood. Activations shift the location of the prior over $s_i$; they do not independently change its spread. This is an activation-conditioned prior construction, not a claim that activations themselves are generated by a noiseless measurement process.

Setting $w = 0$ recovers the baseline exactly. Omit a probe intercept initially: a separate intercept would overlap with latent location and decision bias. Freezing the baseline parameters makes the first comparison easier to interpret.

## Why subtract $\operatorname{logit}(\tau_j)$?

The threshold $\tau_j$ is the event probability above which action 1 is preferable. A bet winning 2 units if the event occurs and losing 1 otherwise has expected payoff $2p - (1-p)$, so its threshold is $1/3$.

The difference $s_i - \operatorname{logit}(\tau_j)$ compares belief with the decision threshold on a common log-odds scale. It is positive when the effective belief exceeds the threshold and negative otherwise. With $\alpha = 0$, action probability is $1/2$ at the threshold; $\beta$ controls the sharpness of the transition. A nonzero $\alpha$ shifts the effective crossing.

The log-odds scale is a modeling assumption inherited from this paper specification. The known threshold lets the same latent belief predict different actions under different stakes.

## Prediction using external responses and activations

Let $O$ be the observed response set and $T$ the withheld target set. The augmented posterior is

$$
p_w(u_i\mid a_{i,O},h_i)
\propto p_0(u_i)\prod_{j\in O}p(a_{ij}\mid u_i,h_i;w).
$$

Predict a withheld binary action by integrating over this posterior:

$$
P_w(a_{it}=1\mid a_{i,O},h_i)
=\int q_{it}(u_i,h_i)\,p_w(u_i\mid a_{i,O},h_i)\,du_i.
$$

The baseline uses the identical observed responses, target responses, and likelihood parameters, with $w = 0$. Target responses must never enter the conditioning posterior.

This supplements the original inference procedure. In contrast, setting $s_i = w^\top h_i$ directly would replace the latent variable and prevent external responses from updating the belief once $h_i$ is known.

## Fitting: a linear probe, not ordinary least squares

The projection is linear in activations, but its weights are fitted by regularized conditional maximum likelihood. For fixed evidence/target masks, minimize

$$
\mathcal L(w)
=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\frac{1}{|T_i|}\sum_{t\in T_i}
\log P_w(a_{it}\mid a_{i,O_i},h_i)
+\lambda\lVert w\rVert_2^2.
$$

This uses a sum of marginal predictive log losses, aligned with evaluating individual withheld responses. It is not the joint log probability of all target responses. Begin with binary targets to keep the objective and scoring simple.

Ordinary ridge regression would instead need numerical targets, such as posterior mean log-odds inferred from external responses, and minimize squared reconstruction error. That is a useful baseline or initialization, but its objective rewards reconstructing the external estimate rather than improving predictions beyond the available external evidence. It may still denoise that estimate; the limitation is the choice of target, not linearity itself.

The proposed fit directly rewards incremental predictive information. It requires iterative optimization and is not generally convex because it retains and integrates over a latent variable. The LLM remains frozen; gradients are needed only for the small probe fit.

Start at $w = 0$, which is the baseline. Optionally compare a ridge-based initialization, but a ridge estimate of the whole belief should not be assumed to be the correct additive correction without refinement.

## Affordable pilot

1. Start with one locally runnable small model and one domain. CKD is a proposed starting point; the exact checkpoint, precision, and case count remain to be chosen.
2. Preserve the existing test split. Divide training cases into fitting and validation subsets, approximately 80/20. Keep all responses from a case together; group related cases where needed.
3. Fit the paper's shared-threshold baseline on fitting cases, then freeze its prior and response parameters for the layer search.
4. Collect activations once per case. Use a fixed prompt containing case information and the focal proposition, without decision-specific thresholds, payoffs, or answers. Use a fixed final token position and a consistent post-block residual-stream definition.
5. Cache the selected token vector from every layer in one forward pass. Use inference mode and small batches; do not retain full token-by-layer tensors or generate reasoning for this pilot.
6. Search roughly eight evenly spaced layers and four regularization strengths. Fit activation preprocessing on fitting cases only and use a consistently normalized loss. Begin with centering; if scaling is used, fix its rule in advance and learn its statistics on fitting cases only.
7. Use a fixed evidence/target split, or a few predetermined masks, matching the intended test-time task. For each candidate, learn $w$ using fitting cases and select by validation withheld-action log loss.
8. Once the configuration is fixed, refit the baseline on all training cases and refit the selected probe with those baseline parameters frozen. Evaluate once on untouched test cases.

Training fit alone is unsuitable for choosing a layer: thousands of activation dimensions can overfit a few hundred cases. Multiple decisions from one case do not create additional independent activation vectors.

For illustration, $500$ cases $\times\ 32$ layers $\times\ 2{,}560$ dimensions in float16 occupy approximately 82 MB on disk. Model weights and intermediate inference tensors require additional memory. Activation collection should dominate the LLM compute cost; the layer search reuses cached arrays.

Existing behavioral responses are reusable only if checkpoint, prompting, and inference configuration are sufficiently matched to the local model. In particular, do not silently treat API responses and a differently quantized local model as the same experimental system.

## Evaluation and controls

Compare on identical cases and withheld decisions:

- Behavior-only: original posterior using observed external responses.
- Behavior plus activations: augmented posterior using those same responses and the cached activation projection.
- Activation-only ablation: augmented predictions with no external responses in the conditioning set.
- Shuffled-activation control: break case/activation correspondence and rerun the specified fitting and evaluation procedure to assess spurious gains.

Primary metric: mean withheld binary-action log loss. Secondary metrics: Brier score and per-decision AUROC where both classes are present. Use paired differences and bootstrap by case, or by the relevant grouping unit for related cases.

If feasible, reserve an entire framing family from baseline/probe fitting and layer selection, then test transfer to it using its known thresholds. This is a stronger test than predicting new cases of decisions already represented in training. It should be a separately specified evaluation, not selected after inspecting test results.

The first result of interest is a reliable improvement over behavior-only prediction. Improvement over activation-only prediction additionally indicates that the two evidence sources are useful together. A null result is specific to the chosen model, prompt, extraction position, linear direction, and prior-shift specification.

## Implementation notes and later extensions

The repository already contains the relevant likelihood and quadrature code:

- `latent_beliefs/holdout/common.py`: shared-threshold behavioral model.
- `latent_beliefs/latent/model.py`: likelihoods, prior weights, and posterior integration.

Retain the baseline quadrature nodes and prior weights. If nodes are stored as probabilities $z_q$, compute $u_q = \operatorname{logit}(z_q)$, then evaluate likelihoods at $\operatorname{sigmoid}(u_q + w^\top \widetilde h_i)$. Existing weights already represent the original prior integration measure; do not introduce an extra Jacobian when simply evaluating shifted likelihoods on those nodes. Perform posterior normalization and predictive integration in log space. Verify numerical stability for large shifts and check quadrature accuracy for the selected fit.

Useful implementation checks are exact recovery of baseline predictions at $w = 0$ and invariance of a prediction to changes in withheld target values when conditioning inputs are fixed.

Defer additional complexity until this pilot is informative: activation-dependent uncertainty, joint refitting of behavioral parameters, multiple directions, multiple domains, and model-size comparisons. These are extensions rather than requirements for the initial deterministic-shift experiment.
